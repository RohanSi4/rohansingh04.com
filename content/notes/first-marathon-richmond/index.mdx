I ran my first marathon yesterday: the Allianz Richmond Marathon, Saturday,
November 14, 2026, in Richmond, Virginia. Official chip time was 3:34:43.

The goal was 3:20, which is 7:38 a mile, with sub-3:25 as the floor. I missed
both. Through mile 24 I was right on 3:25 pace, and then around mile 25 both of
my hamstrings cramped and the last mile and a quarter turned into something else
entirely. I'm still happy with it. Mostly. Let me explain.

## the build-up

I only started running consistently this spring and summer. My peak training
week was about 43 miles in mid August, and my longest training run was 20.5
miles. Then at the end of August I moved from San Jose to Charlottesville, which
is a lot hillier and a lot more humid. Every easy run suddenly had opinions.

The last stretch was not clean. I caught a cold in late October, then ran a
fever on November 2, and that ate into the weeks that were supposed to sharpen
me up. Over the final 8 weeks my base averaged about 20 miles a week, which is
light for a marathon, and I knew it.

There was one good sign. A week before the race I ran a 10K in 42:59, which
suggested roughly 3:18 marathon fitness. So the engine looked like it was
there. The open question was whether my legs would agree for 26.2 miles.

## race day

Breakfast was at 4am: bagels with preserves, a banana, and Gatorade. Big,
boring, and exactly what I had practiced. No coffee that morning. I was saving
the caffeine for later.

The weather was close to ideal. About 32°F and frosty at the 7:00am start,
around 45°F at the finish. You don't get to complain about that.

I lined up with the 3:25 pace group and stayed with them for the first 20
miles. That was the whole plan for the first part of the race: let someone else
do the math, stay relaxed, don't get greedy. After 20 I moved ahead of the
group, and through mile 24 I was right on 3:25 pace.

Then, at about mile 25, both hamstrings cramped. Not a twinge. Both legs, at
the same time, done. I had to stop twice in the last mile and a quarter, once
for roughly 3.5 minutes and then again for about 5 minutes, and I jogged it in
from there. All in, that cost me about 8 to 9 minutes.

The frustrating part is that aerobically I felt great. Breathing was fine,
energy was fine. The legs just gave out.

## what went right

Fueling went exactly to plan. A gel at miles 4, 7, 10, 14, and 17, then
caffeinated gels at 20 and 23. I never ran out of energy, and for a first
marathon I will take that.

Early pacing was disciplined. Sitting in the 3:25 group for 20 miles kept me
from going out too fast, which is the classic first marathon mistake and one I
was fully capable of making.

And the coach held up its end. I coached myself with [Marathon Prep
Bot](/projects/marathon-prep-bot), a system I built where Claude Code acts as
the coach and a private repo is the data layer. My Apple Watch feeds HealthFit,
HealthFit feeds the repo, and from there the coach writes the plan, scores
every run, and publishes my training dashboard to the [fitness page](/fitness)
on this site. Back in July I [wrote about how it works](/notes/llm-marathon-coach)
and promised to report back after the race. This is that report. It got me to
the start line after a cold and a fever with enough fitness to run 24 good
miles, and it kept the plan honest when the last few weeks went sideways.

## what I would change

The engine was there. The legs needed more durability. That is the whole
lesson, and it points at three things.

- **More long-run volume.** One 20.5 mile run was not enough. Next time I want
  several 20 to 22 mile runs, with the last miles at marathon pace, so my legs
  already know what the end of a marathon feels like at speed.
- **Year round hamstring strength work.** Not a few weeks of it before a race.
  All year.
- **Respecting the heart-rate trigger.** There was a trigger at mile 16 that
  said to ease off. Next time I respect it. Building a coach and then not
  listening to it is a pretty funny way to lose an argument with yourself.

## what's next

The Charlottesville Marathon in spring 2027, where I find out whether all the
hills I have been complaining about were secretly training. After that, I am
building toward a half Ironman in summer 2027.

3:34:43 is not the number I wanted. But it's my first marathon, it's on the
board, and I know what to fix. The coach already has notes.
